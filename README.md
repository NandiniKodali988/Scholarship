# Conflict and Compassion: A Deeper Dive into Attacks on Aid Workers

## Overview

This project explores over 25 years of global attacks on humanitarian aid workers, providing an interactive, data-driven narrative of how violence against aid workers has escalated across regions, actors, and conflict context. 

The goal is to deliver actionable insights for humanitarian organizations and security analysts by examining trends, regional variations, perpetrators, and attack methods. 

## Conclusions

Violence against humanitarian workers has increased notably since the early 2000s, wih major spikes aligning wih conflicts in Afghanistan, Syria, and South Sudan. The majority of the known attacks are attributed to Non-State armed Groups, primarily through ambushes, raids, and individual attacks in high-risk zones. 

INGOs bear the highest overall burden (3,497 affected) , while NNGOs face heightened risks in countries like Syria (53.4%) and Afghanistan (32.8%), often without adequate security infrastructure. 

Threat patterns vary by region — State Actors are dominant in Palestine and Syria, while NSAGs lead in Somalia, Sudan and DR Congo. This variation underscore the need for tailored, context-specific security strategies, rather than a uniform approach.
